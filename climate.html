What heat, haze and sun do to your skin

Singapore's weather is tough on skin, and it's getting hotter: 2015 to 2025 were the 11 warmest years ever recorded worldwide.1 Here's what that means for breakouts, dryness and dark spots, and what helps.

Heat: more oil, more breakouts, drier skin

As temperatures rise, prolonged heat increases sweating and oil production, which clogs pores and leads to breakouts.

Heat, and the dry air-conditioning we retreat into, can also strain the skin's barrier, leaving it dehydrated, irritated and more vulnerable. Scientists are still working out exactly how temperature affects the water skin loses, but air pollution has consistently been shown to increase it.5 What was once an occasional problem is becoming part of everyday life.

Haze and pollution: dullness and early ageing

Rising temperatures can worsen air pollution. Pollutants such as particulate matter (PM), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), ozone (O₃) and soot cause oxidative stress in the skin.2,3

Every day, particles from vehicles, industry and city life settle on your skin and generate free radicals: unstable molecules that damage collagen and elastin, the proteins that keep skin firm. Research has linked traffic-related air pollution to premature ageing, wrinkles, dark spots and inflammation.2,3

Dullness

Free radicals and pollutant build-up leave skin looking tired and uneven.

Sensitivity

A weakened barrier reacts more easily to heat, products and the environment.

Early ageing

Damaged collagen and elastin show up as fine lines and loss of firmness.

Sun: dark spots and fine lines

The World Health Organization notes that rising temperatures, air pollution and environmental change can increase our exposure to harmful UV radiation.4

UV is one of the leading causes of photoageing: fine lines, dark spots, uneven tone and loss of elasticity.2 Combined with pollution and heat, it puts even more pressure on the skin's ability to repair itself.

That's why a simple daily routine of cleansing, hydration and SPF does more for your skin than any single product.
